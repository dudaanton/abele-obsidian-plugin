# Quick button

A round button at the edge of a phone's screen that opens a menu of quick actions for whatever
you are looking at — a note, a book, a chat, a GitHub tab. It is a concept and starts switched
off: turn it on in **Settings → Abele → Quick button**.

## The menu

A tap opens the menu from the bottom of the screen, in three groups:

- **Your actions** — the commands and scripts you put there in the settings, then the note's
  own [header buttons](scripts#header-buttons). A command is offered only where it can run, so
  one meant for notes is not offered over a book.
- **What this is** — what the plugin offers for the thing in front: the timer and a chat about
  the note or one attached to it; in a book, the link to the place, a chat about it, drawing on
  a PDF, the search, highlights, bookmarks and the text and layout; in a GitHub tab, another
  item or a chat about this one; in the chat, a new chat.
- **Command palette** — the way to everything else.

The same menu opens with the **Open quick menu** command, with or without the button, and on a
computer too.

## Where it stands

At the right edge by default, just above whatever is at the bottom of the screen — Obsidian's
navigation bar, the line under a book's page, the chat's message box — so it never covers a
control. Drag it to move it: let go and it goes to the nearer edge and keeps the height you
left it at, on every screen and on your other devices. **Put it back** in the settings brings
it down again.

## When it gets out of the way

While you read it slides to the edge with a sliver still showing, which is enough to tap: when
you scroll down, and back when you scroll up or switch tabs. In a book, where the text reaches
the edges of the screen, it always rests that way, above the line under the page.

It is not there at all while the keyboard is up, while words are selected, while a dialog is
open, while a sidebar other than the chat covers the screen, and in a book while you draw on the
pages, look at a picture full screen, have the contents open or a highlight's bar showing.

## Your actions

**Add a command** searches every command Obsidian has, other plugins' included; **Add a
script** searches your [scripts](scripts). Each can have a name and an icon of its own for the
menu, and the arrows set the order. Taking one out of the menu does not touch the command or
script itself.
